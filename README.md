# Training portfolio

This is a collection of training courses I have designed and taught for new hires, support teams and technical staff. For each course you can see the plan, the lesson, the quiz and the scoring guide, and how I checked that people really learned. I am a QA analyst with a background in customer support and training. Every company, system and person in these materials is made up, so nothing here comes from an employer.

## In plain English

Training is how a new person becomes useful quickly. A good course tells you who it is for and what you will be able to do at the end. It shows how the trainer teaches it, how you check that people understood, and what to change next time. This portfolio shows all of those parts, not only the slides.

## At a glance

## The courses

### QA automation onboarding

*For testers who are new to automation tools. 10 days, 1 hour a day. About a ten-minute read.*

After ten one-hour days, a tester who has never written code can open a project in VS Code (a free tool for writing code), ask an AI assistant for help, and write and run a simple automated test of a website. Two of the days are hands-on, with no video, because the goal is to do, not only watch.

The layout is in the [course page template](docs/templates/course-page-template.md) and the [lesson plan template](docs/templates/lesson-plan-template.md).

## How I check that people learned

- **During the lesson:** a quick check at the end of every step (a question, a short exercise, a show of hands), so someone who is lost is found in minutes, not weeks.
- **At the end:** a marked assignment, a quiz, and a practice task scored with the scoring guide.
- **Later:** one question a month on, "have you used this since?" A good score on the day shows people understood. It does not show that their work changed.
- **After each run:** the feedback and the common mistakes become the first change for the next run, written at the bottom of the course page.

## Words used on this page

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Onboarding | The first weeks of a new person: what they learn, and when |
| Lesson plan | A one-page plan for a session: steps, timings, checks |
| Scoring guide (rubric) | A guide that says what each score looks like, so two trainers mark the same way |
| Benchmark | A task a learner must do to show they have reached a stage |
| Enablement | Training and materials that help a team do its work well |
| Go-live | The day a customer or team starts using a system for real work |
| Runbook | Step-by-step instructions for handling a known situation |
